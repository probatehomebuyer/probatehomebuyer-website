# Stage 1 competitor-led SEO and redesign review

**Research completed:** 3 August 2026 (United Kingdom)  
**Scope:** live organic results and seller-facing websites relevant to Probate Home Buyer; paid placements were not included. Rankings are a dated observation, not a claim of stable positions.

## Executive conclusion

The results are fragmented. Direct buyers compete most strongly on transactional inherited-property searches, while solicitors, estate agents, auction businesses, GOV.UK and editorial property sites dominate legal and process questions. The closest commercial benchmarks are The Property Buying Company, LDN Properties, Property Saviour, House Buy Fast, Bettermove, Sold.co.uk, Property Solvers and probate specialist Sail Homes.

PR #6 already moves Probate Home Buyer in the right direction: a shorter seller-led headline, one postcode field, an obvious phone action, simpler navigation, calmer hierarchy and probate-specific routes. The justified pre-merge refinement is deliberately small: simplify the homepage hero and make the form/header more robust at narrow widths. Larger trust work should follow only when evidence can be verified, especially transparent offer methodology, fee/deduction wording, third-party memberships or reviews, and stronger anonymised purchase evidence.

## Method and classification

The searches below were run on 3 August 2026 using UK-qualified wording. For each query, the table records leading organic pages actually returned during the review. Search results vary by device, location, history and engine; the order shown is observational and should not be treated as a permanent ranking. “Schema” is recorded only where it could be observed or inferred from a rendered rich-result pattern; absence means not confirmed, not necessarily absent.

Categories used:

- **Direct cash property buyer:** buys directly or presents a direct/rapid buyer route.
- **Estate agent / hybrid seller service:** markets property or combines buyer-network and agency routes.
- **Auction company:** sells by auction.
- **Probate solicitor / specialist:** legal, estate-administration or probate-specific service.
- **Government / authoritative:** official public guidance or high-authority consumer property guidance.
- **Editorial / general property:** informational publisher without a probate-purchase proposition on the ranking page.

## Ranking-page patterns

### Titles and meta-description approach

Commercial winners normally put the task first: “Selling an Inherited Property,” “Selling a Probate Property,” or “Sell Your House After Probate.” Snippets then add a differentiator—cash route, no repairs, probate steps, fees or a timeframe. Informational winners use an exact question and answer it immediately. The revised PR #6 H1, **Sell a Probate Property Fast**, is direct and natural; the existing title tag remains broader and should not be changed casually before performance is monitored.

### Depth, headings and FAQs

The strongest informational pages are substantially deeper than a sales landing page. They use a quick answer or key takeaways, a contents list, sequential H2s, narrow H3s, FAQs and contextual links. Commercial pages often repeat benefit blocks and CTAs, which increases conversion opportunities but can create clutter. Probate Home Buyer should keep a clean commercial homepage and let the guide/article cluster carry depth.

FAQ structured data appears to be a common implementation opportunity, but rich-result visibility was not consistently observable from the result set and Google does not guarantee FAQ display. Existing valid Article/FAQ schema should be preserved rather than multiplied.

### Publication and update signals

Named authors, reviewer credentials and explicit “last reviewed” dates are prominent on Ready Steady Sell, Property Solvers, The Property Buying Company and solicitor content. Other commercial landing pages show no useful date. Probate Home Buyer’s visible **Posted** convention is consistent and should remain; a later editorial-governance stage can add a genuine expert-review process without presenting an artificial update date.

### Internal linking

Large competitors connect inherited-property guides to probate sale, tax, fast-sale, local market and valuation pages. This builds depth but sometimes funnels every paragraph back to a commercial exact-match page. Probate Home Buyer’s more restrained separation—informational articles, guide hub and commercial service pages—is strategically sound. The next opportunity is contextual executor-focused links, not adding more navigation choices to the homepage.

### Postcode-first versus long-form capture

Postcode-first capture is used by several strong commercial competitors and is the right pattern for PR #6. Long first-screen forms collect richer leads but add privacy and effort at the moment of least trust. Probate Home Buyer should keep the postcode-only GET step, retain the value on the next page and explain subsequent information requirements there.

### Mobile headers, calls and cookie impact

The common effective pattern is a compact logo, one phone control and one menu control. Some competitor pages combine phone numbers, ratings, forms, floating chat and cookie notices, leaving little uninterrupted first-screen space. PR #6’s compact phone/menu pairing and reduced cookie treatment are preferable. A floating contact control should not be added in Stage 1.

### Offer calculation, fees and deductions

Bettermove describes valuation inputs; House Buy Fast acknowledges below-market offers; Property Saviour explicitly discusses its offer percentage and executor duty. This is a material trust theme. Probate Home Buyer should later explain, in plain English, that a direct offer accounts for condition, resale risk, comparable evidence, works and transaction costs, and should make the price/certainty trade-off explicit. Exact percentages or savings must not be published without an approved commercial policy and evidence.

### Executor and beneficiary reassurance

Strong pages distinguish preparatory steps from exchange/completion and discuss multiple beneficiaries, insurance, holding costs and record-keeping. Probate Home Buyer already addresses these subjects across the approved routes. It should avoid turning the homepage into legal guidance; concise links to the executor checklist, empty-home guide and probate guide are the better pattern.

## Strengths worth learning from

1. Immediate task match in the H1 and snippet.
2. One low-friction valuation or offer start, often postcode-first.
3. Transparent explanation of the route, offer inputs, fees and trade-offs.
4. Named authors/reviewers, visible review governance and source citations on informational pages.
5. Verifiable third-party reputation, regulatory/membership links and company identity.
6. Real case evidence showing property condition, route chosen and outcome without exposing private client data.
7. Clear distinctions between marketing, exchange and completion around probate authority.

## Common competitor weaknesses

- Dense first screens with several promises, trust strips, forms, chat widgets and repeated CTAs.
- Long forms before the user understands who will receive their details.
- Precise speed, cost, fall-through or transaction claims without visible methodology.
- Emotional or fear-led copy that can feel inappropriate for bereaved families.
- Ambiguous “we buy” language where the business may broker, market or use an investor network.
- Excessively long articles that repeat commercial keywords and dilute the practical answer.
- Inconsistent legal shorthand about “selling” before probate, especially where marketing, exchange and completion are not separated.

## Specific implications for PR #6

### Already addressed

- Seller intent is understood immediately.
- The hero is calmer than the typical direct-buyer page.
- Postcode-first capture reduces friction.
- Navigation gives priority to sale, process, guidance, experience and contact.
- Mobile phone and menu actions remain visible without a second floating control.
- The selected service and article pages separate commercial and informational intent.
- The design retains cautious probate wording and does not invent speed or outcome claims.

### Low-risk changes justified before merge

1. Use the shorter approved hero: eyebrow **PROBATE PROPERTY BUYERS**, H1 **Sell a Probate Property Fast**, and one supporting sentence.
2. Use **Get My Offer** and a single reassurance line; remove the overlapping mini-proof row.
3. Reduce hero height and stack the postcode field/button below 480px while preserving one-line desktop layout.
4. Keep CTA text unwrapped, make phone/menu targets at least 44px, preserve visible focus, and compact the mobile cookie banner.
5. Keep Stage 1 CSS scoped so untouched page families do not inherit the redesign.

No competitor finding justifies a new homepage section, new claim, review count, fixed timeframe, transaction statistic or structural redesign before approval.

## Recommended later-stage improvements

### Priority 1: evidence and transparency

- Publish an approved “how we calculate a direct offer” explanation, including the open-market trade-off and any deductions or costs.
- Strengthen case studies with genuine, anonymised property condition, seller priority, route and outcome evidence.
- Link any genuine review profile, NAPB/TPO membership, proof-of-funds process or professional credential only after verification.
- Add a clear business-model statement wherever a user might otherwise wonder whether Probate Home Buyer buys directly or introduces another buyer.

### Priority 2: editorial authority

- Establish named author/reviewer ownership and a real review schedule for legal/probate guidance.
- Cite GOV.UK, HMRC, Land Registry and relevant insurance/council sources where claims depend on current rules.
- Expand the empty-house page around insurance notification, security checks and council-tax signposting without turning it into regulated advice.
- Monitor query-to-page Search Console evidence to protect the separation between informational articles and commercial service pages.

### Priority 3: conversion learning

- Measure postcode-start to completed-enquiry progression before adding fields or extra first-screen proof.
- Test clarity of the next step and privacy reassurance on the offer page, not a longer homepage form.
- Consider a concise route-comparison module later, clearly presented as factual differences rather than independent advice.

## Authority and backlink observations

Authority was assessed qualitatively from visible evidence only. GOV.UK has primary authority for the probate process. Solicitor pages benefit from named qualified professionals; Zoopla and HomeOwners Alliance have established consumer-property authority; Savills has a strong national property brand. Among direct/hybrid competitors, visible trust categories include third-party reviews, NAPB/TPO references, proof-of-funds statements, long trading history, named authors, media mentions and broad probate content clusters.

No paid backlink index was available in this review, so backlink counts, domain-authority scores and traffic estimates are intentionally omitted. Potentially useful backlink categories—not claims about links already held—include solicitor/executor resources, bereavement support directories, reputable property publications, local professional partners, consumer-protection bodies and genuinely newsworthy market research. Any outreach should earn citations through useful evidence rather than reciprocal or paid link schemes.

## Research limitations

- Organic ordering can change and may differ by location, device, history and search provider.
- The result set is a structured sample, not a complete market census.
- Search Console query data is aggregated and omits some low-volume/private rows; the review uses only the themes already disclosed in the repository audit.
- No reliable third-party search-volume, traffic, conversion or backlink dataset was available. None is estimated.
- Some pages load schema, reviews or interface elements dynamically; schema absence was not inferred from a text-only crawl.
- Competitor claims were recorded as visible claims, not independently verified facts.
- Mobile and performance observations are directional unless separately measured in the PR #6 QA report.
